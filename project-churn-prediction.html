Disclaimer

This project was completed for educational and analytical purposes using synthetic data. The findings and model performance are illustrative and should not be interpreted as actual business results or predictions about real customers.

Project Background

Customer churn presents a retention challenge for residential broadband providers, particularly when intervention begins only after customers decide to leave. This project explored how machine learning could help identify customers with higher churn likelihood and prioritize retention resources based on both predicted risk and potential revenue exposure.

As part of the RevoU × Telkom Indonesia virtual internship, I developed a churn prediction workflow using a synthetic residential broadband dataset covering DKI Jakarta. The objective was to move beyond identifying churn patterns and build a predictive approach that supports proactive, revenue-aware customer retention.

Methodology

The project followed an end-to-end machine learning workflow, including data preparation, exploratory data analysis, feature engineering, model comparison, hyperparameter tuning, model evaluation, and SHAP-based explainability.

MDLC 1 — Baseline Modeling

Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost were evaluated using cross-validation. XGBoost was selected as the baseline model, achieving a CV ROC-AUC of 0.980.

MDLC 2 — In-Context Learning

The workflow was extended by grouping features according to category and actionability, then applying an XGBoost teacher–student distillation approach. The resulting XGBoost (IC) model achieved a ROC-AUC of 0.956. SHAP and feature validation were used to interpret churn-related signals and connect model outputs with potential business actions.

Results

The MDLC 2 scoring workflow identified 339 high-risk customers with a predicted churn probability of at least 19.22% and monthly fees above the median. This revenue-based prioritization was designed to focus retention efforts on customers with both elevated churn risk and higher financial value.

Leading Churn-Related Signals

  • Lower average active days over three months
  • Lower average network quality over three months
  • Longer average complaint resolution time over six months
  • Lower usage ratio
  • Higher downtime over three months

These signals informed recommendations for proactive retention, network-quality improvement, complaint-resolution escalation, customer engagement recovery, and incentives to encourage longer-term contracts.
